# Case Study: Enterprise Nostro Cash Reconciliation & Automated Break Remediation

## 1. Institutional Context & Business Problem

In global investment banks and institutional broker-dealers, capital markets trading desks execute hundreds of thousands of multi-currency transactions daily. Cash settlement for these trades flows through a global network of correspondent bank accounts, termed **Nostro accounts** (*"our money held at your bank"*).

### The Operational Challenge
Every morning, Financial Control, Cash Operations, and Product Control teams must reconcile two massive datasets:
1. **The Internal General Ledger (GL):** Real-time booking records generated by trading systems (Murex, Calypso, internal blotters) reflecting expected cash inflows and outflows.
2. **External Bank Statements:** Official cash statements received via SWIFT MT940 or ISO 20022 XML (`camt.053`) files from correspondent custodians (e.g., JPMorgan Chase NY, Deutsche Bank Frankfurt, Barclays London, State Bank of India Mumbai, MUFG Tokyo).

When millions of dollars move across international borders, discrepancies inevitably arise due to:
- Cross-border time zone differences and clearing cut-offs (Fedwire vs. TARGET2 vs. CHAPS vs. RTGS).
- Correspondent intermediary bank cable fees deducted silently from principal proceeds.
- Micro-rounding discrepancies in FX conversion rates between trade booking and bank fixing.
- Unilateral execution: trades booked in the front office that failed at the clearing house, or counterparty wire transfers received without a corresponding internal trade.

### The Financial & Regulatory Risk
Unreconciled items—termed **"breaks"**—present acute operational, credit, and regulatory hazards:
- **SOX Section 404 (Internal Controls over Financial Reporting):** Material unreconciled cash accounts can lead to significant control deficiencies or material weaknesses, subjecting the CFO to disclosure penalties.
- **BCBS 239 (Risk Data Aggregation & Reporting):** Regulators require global banks to aggregate intraday liquidity and counterparty exposures in real time. Aged breaks distort net liquidity calculations.
- **Cost of Manual Triage:** At scale, large banks employ hundreds of operations analysts manually sifting through Excel sheets to find matching wire references—a slow, error-prone, and non-scalable practice.

## 2. Solution Architecture: The Deterministic 4-Pass Waterfall

To replace fragile manual spreadsheet ticking, we engineered a deterministic, high-throughput matching engine designed with four distinct evaluation passes.

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                9,695 Raw Ledger Records                                 |
|            5,000 Internal GL Records  |  4,695 External Bank Statement Records          |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                           |
                                           v
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| PASS 1: Exact Key Matching (84.0% Matches / 3,892 pairs)                                |
| Criteria: Exact Account ID + Normalized Reference + Net Amount + Exact Value Date       |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                           |  (Unmatched Records)
                                           v
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| PASS 2: Tolerance & Fee Strip Matching (8.6% Matches / 398 pairs)                       |
| Criteria: Same Ref + Date, Amount within $0.05 or Known Correspondent Cable Fee ($15-45)|
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                           |  (Unmatched Records)
                                           v
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| PASS 3: Settlement Window Lag Matching (5.6% Matches / 261 pairs)                       |
| Criteria: Exact Ref + Exact Amount, Value Date offset within +/- 2 calendar days        |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                           |  (Unmatched Records)
                                           v
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| PASS 4: Normalized Levenshtein Fuzzy Matching (1.8% Matches / 82 pairs)                 |
| Criteria: String similarity >= 85% on normalized wire tokens + Same Amount & Date       |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                           |
                                           v
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| RESIDUAL EXCEPTIONS (354 breaks | $263,774,940.20 Gross Exposure)                       |
| Root-Cause Classification -> SOX Ageing Buckets -> Balanced Remediation Journal Entry   |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Why a Waterfall Rather Than Pure Fuzzy Matching?
A common rookie mistake in financial automation is applying loose fuzzy matching across all fields simultaneously. In an institutional banking environment, this creates disastrous **false positive matches**—matching an unrelated \$100,000 corporate bond interest payment with an unrelated \$100,000 repo margin call. 

By executing a **strict deterministic waterfall**:
- Pass 1 eliminates 84% of high-volume, clean transactions with 100% mathematical certainty.
- Pass 2 isolates predictable friction (penny rounding and standard SWIFT cable charges) without relaxing reference uniqueness.
- Pass 3 permits date drift *only* when the reference and currency amount are identical.
- Pass 4 restricts fuzzy string matching *only* to records where currency, value date, and exact monetary amount already match, ensuring zero cross-trade contamination.

## 3. Forensic Break Classification & Ageing Taxonomy

The engine passes all 354 residual breaks through a rule-based diagnostic classifier. Each break is assigned a root-cause classification and placed into regulatory ageing buckets:

```
+-------------------+---------------+--------------------+--------------------------------+
| Ageing Bucket     | Break Count   | Gross Exposure USD | Regulatory Severity            |
+-------------------+---------------+--------------------+--------------------------------+
| 0 - 30 Days       |      156      |    $82,884,140.10  | Normal Operating Float         |
| 31 - 60 Days      |      114      |    $83,745,200.00  | Secondary Supervisory Review   |
| 61 - 90 Days      |       73      |    $76,645,600.10  | Escalated SOX Review           |
| > 90 Days         |       11      |    $20,500,000.00  | CRITICAL SOX 404 Materiality   |
+-------------------+---------------+--------------------+--------------------------------+
| Total             |      354      |   $263,774,940.20  |                                |
+-------------------+---------------+--------------------+--------------------------------+
```

### Root Cause Categorization
1. **UNILATERAL_BANK_SIDE (120 breaks | $89.45M):** External bank statement reflects money moved or received, but no internal trade was booked. Typical scenario: counterparty wire sent with ambiguous reference or custodian interest credit unbooked.
2. **UNILATERAL_LEDGER_SIDE (108 breaks | $78.21M):** Internal GL reflects expected movement, but statement shows zero activity. Typical scenario: trade settlement failed at the central depository or counterparty default.
3. **TIMING_CUTOFF_LAG (58 breaks | $46.12M):** Legitimate payments booked at month-end where settlement spanned more than 2 business days due to holiday cross-overs.
4. **FX_SLIPPAGE_DISCREPANCY (32 breaks | $24.89M):** Currency settlements where front-office booked at indicative fixing while correspondent bank applied executed spot rate.
5. **UNPOSTED_BANK_FEE (24 breaks | $1,878.50):** Small cable, SWIFT, and custodian charges deducted from gross wire amounts without prior ledger pre-advice.
6. **ROUNDING_VARIANCE_EXCEEDED (12 breaks | $25.10M):** Multi-currency conversion discrepancies exceeding the $\$0.05$ threshold requiring manual Middle-Office reconciliation.

## 4. Remediation: Automated Double-Entry Adjusting Journal Voucher

Identifying breaks is only half the battle; the month-end close requires **adjusting journal entries** to true up the GL before financial statements are published.

The engine automatically synthesizes 234 balanced accounting lines in `data/output/adjusting_journal_entry.csv`, observing institutional double-entry rules:

- **Unposted Bank Fees:**
  - `Debit:` Bank Charges Expense (`GL 810010`)
  - `Credit:` Cash at Bank Nostro (`GL 110010`)
- **Unilateral Bank Credits (Unidentified Deposits):**
  - `Debit:` Cash at Bank Nostro (`GL 110010`)
  - `Credit:` Bank Clearing Suspense Account (`GL 290010`)
- **Unilateral Ledger Failures (Pending Trade Settlements):**
  - `Debit:` Trade Settlement Suspense (`GL 190010`)
  - `Credit:` Cash at Bank Nostro (`GL 110010`)
- **FX Discrepancies:**
  - `Debit/Credit:` Realized FX Gain/Loss (`GL 720010`)
  - Offset to Cash at Bank Nostro (`GL 110010`)

### Proof of Accounting Balance
$$\sum \text{Debits} = \$51,431,104.89$$
$$\sum \text{Credits} = \$51,431,104.89$$
$$\text{Net Journal Imbalance} = \mathbf{\$0.0000}$$

Every adjusting line preserves full lineage, documenting the parent Nostro Account, Transaction Reference, Break Category, and specific Reason Code.

## 5. Measured Business Impact

1. **Efficiency:** Replaces manual two-way ticking across 9,695 items with an automated pipeline executing in under 2 seconds.
2. **Match Coverage:** Successfully matches **95.13% of transactions** automatically, allowing operations staff to focus 100% of their analytical capacity on the 354 genuine exceptions.
3. **Audit Defense:** Generates complete audit trails required by external statutory auditors (e.g. PwC, EY, Deloitte, KPMG) and internal SOX 404 control reviewers.
4. **Intraday Liquidity Optimization:** Gives treasury real-time visibility into the exact currency and aging profile of trapped liquidity across global accounts.
